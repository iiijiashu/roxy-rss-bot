## Today's Highlights
Security and reliability are dominating the AI conversation today, particularly concerning prompt injection and agent exploitation. Developers are actively debating whether AI coding agents can be trusted to verify their own work, such as running tests or maintaining code integrity. There is growing skepticism toward "vibe coding" and simple certifications, with practitioners emphasizing the need for rigorous audits and human-in-the-loop designs. Enterprise environments face new threats as attackers weaponize AI agents with broad access, prompting calls for stricter sandboxing and data privacy measures like homomorphic encryption.

## Community Pulse
Developers are increasingly focused on the **security and trustworthiness of AI agents**. There is a strong consensus that prompt injection and agent exploitation represent critical risks, particularly in enterprise environments where AI has broad access to systems like CRMs. Many posts highlight the gap between what agents claim to do (e.g., "tests pass") and their actual execution, prompting a shift toward rigorous, independent verification methods.

Practitioners are also moving beyond basic "vibe coding" to explore more structured patterns, such as adversarial agent debates and human-in-the-loop designs for voice agents. The rise of plugin ecosystems for AI agents mirrors the early npm era, with new supply-chain security vulnerabilities emerging. On the hardware side, there is growing interest in local and efficient AI models, including those that can be trained on consumer laptops, suggesting a democratization of advanced ML techniques.
