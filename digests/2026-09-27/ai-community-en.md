### Community Pulse
The AI communities are currently grappling with the "black box" of autonomous agents, particularly regarding security and verification. A dominant theme on Dev.to is the shift from "writing code" to "auditing AI," with developers questioning if they are just rubber-stamping automated pull requests. There is a growing skepticism about prompt engineering as a primary skill, with many arguing that system architecture and documentation are the new hard skills.

Practical concerns are moving toward the infrastructure layer, where developers are reporting insecure MCP server defaults and seeking better ways to manage agent memory without data corruption. The discussion is also heavily focused on local AI capabilities, with users testing the limits of 8GB VRAM and Mac unified memory. On Lobsters, the conversation is more critical of the AI giants themselves, with a focus on privacy leaks (ChatGPT tracking) and the security risks of autonomous agent "hacks." The overall pulse suggests a maturation phase where the hype is cooling and the focus is shifting to reliability, security, and professional verification standards.
